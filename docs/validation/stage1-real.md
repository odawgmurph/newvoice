# Stage 1 validation: real recordings

Generated by `cd harness && uv run python -m validation.report_real` with newvoice-core 0.0.1. Do not edit by hand. Synthetic-corpus results, with known ground truth, are in [`stage1-synthetic.md`](stage1-synthetic.md).

## Pitch on read speech (LibriSpeech)

20 utterances, 10 men and 10 women, 3.5–6 s each, from LibriSpeech dev-clean (CC BY 4.0, committed in `harness/corpus/librispeech/`). There is no ground truth, so Praat `To Pitch (ac)` on the clean recording is the reference; degraded copies are scored against it, and Praat's own row shows what each degradation costs a reference tracker.

- `clean`: as recorded
- `snr20`: white noise at 20 dB SNR
- `snr10`: white noise at 10 dB SNR
- `phone`: telephone channel: 300-3400 Hz band-pass, 8 kHz, 8-bit mu-law

Within 1% and gross errors (>20% off, mostly octave jumps) are shares of frames both trackers call voiced; recall is the share of reference-voiced frames called voiced, false voicing the share of reference-unvoiced frames called voiced. Regression gates in `tests/test_real_speech.py`.

| Condition | Tracker | Within 1% (%) | Gross (%) | Recall (%) | False voicing (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| clean | YIN | 93.2 | 0.56 | 76.9 | 4.3 |
| clean | MPM | 84.1 | 0.79 | 78.4 | 4.3 |
| clean | pYIN | 88.6 | 0.18 | 92.2 | 13.5 |
| snr20 | Praat | 96.9 | 0.17 | 96.0 | 2.3 |
| snr20 | YIN | 92.8 | 0.50 | 74.0 | 0.1 |
| snr20 | MPM | 84.4 | 0.71 | 75.2 | 0.5 |
| snr20 | pYIN | 87.2 | 0.09 | 91.4 | 10.9 |
| snr10 | Praat | 92.3 | 0.24 | 85.6 | 0.1 |
| snr10 | YIN | 93.4 | 0.07 | 59.9 | 0.0 |
| snr10 | MPM | 86.9 | 0.72 | 62.1 | 0.0 |
| snr10 | pYIN | 81.5 | 0.34 | 86.6 | 5.8 |
| phone | Praat | 84.7 | 10.08 | 83.5 | 3.8 |
| phone | YIN | 83.5 | 11.93 | 60.5 | 3.9 |
| phone | MPM | 75.1 | 15.60 | 61.3 | 3.6 |
| phone | pYIN | 83.6 | 8.66 | 69.7 | 5.9 |

On real speech the 1% frame-level match depends on more than accuracy: Praat agrees with its own clean track on only about 97% of frames once 20 dB of noise is added, and frames at voicing edges differ between any two trackers. Before decision D11 the 5-frame median filter cut YIN's within-1% share from 93% to 83% by clipping contour peaks; it now only replaces outliers. pYIN finds the most voiced frames and makes the fewest gross errors except at 10 dB SNR, where YIN, voicing far fewer frames, makes fewer; pYIN's extra frames are mostly at voicing edges (about two thirds within 50 ms of Praat-voiced speech), the rest low-level breath just above the −60 dBFS gate. The telephone channel removes most men's fundamentals, and every tracker, Praat included, makes about 10% gross errors there.

## Vowels with hand-checked formants (Hillenbrand 1995)

540 tokens from 45 men and 576 from 48 women, h-V-d words at 16 kHz, with Hillenbrand's hand-corrected LPC formants and F0 at the judges' steady-state time. The recordings come from Santiago Barreda's mirror (hosted with Jim Hillenbrand's permission), pinned by SHA-256 and downloaded into `harness/.cache`; they are not committed. Gates in `tests/test_hillenbrand.py`.

### Formants

newvoice-core's sustained-vowel sweep with known boundaries, over 80 ms centred on the steady-state time (like for like), and over the middle half of the vowel nucleus (as the app measures a prompted vowel). Median error and share within 10%, F1 / F2 / F3.

| Window | Group | Tokens | Median error (%) | Within 10% (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| steady state | men | 525 | 1.8 / 1.4 / 0.9 | 97.7 / 97.5 / 94.1 |
| steady state | women | 573 | 1.9 / 1.5 / 0.9 | 96.5 / 93.7 / 92.0 |
| nucleus | men | 540 | 2.8 / 3.2 / 1.4 | 89.8 / 90.6 / 93.3 |
| nucleus | women | 576 | 3.8 / 3.4 / 1.3 | 79.2 / 81.8 / 93.9 |

The gap between the two windows is mostly vowel-inherent spectral change (/ɪ e æ ʊ/ glide in American English), not tracking error: Hillenbrand measured one instant, the nucleus window takes a median over its middle half.

### Bandwidth filter

Share within 10% (F1 / F2 / F3) over the nucleus, with poles wider than the cutoff dropped before numbering (decision D8).

| Cutoff | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|
| 600 Hz | 90.2 / 90.6 / 91.3 | 78.8 / 82.6 / 92.7 |
| 1000 Hz (default) | 89.8 / 90.6 / 93.3 | 79.2 / 81.8 / 93.9 |
| 1500 Hz | 90.0 / 90.6 / 94.3 | 78.8 / 81.1 / 93.9 |
| none (Praat raw) | 90.4 / 90.4 / 95.0 | 79.3 / 79.3 / 90.8 |

Any cutoff from 600 to 1500 Hz does about equally well; no filter loses 2–3 points on women's F2 and F3, where a wide pole takes a formant slot. The 1000 Hz default stays.

### Formant confidence

Fitted on these steady-state tokens (decision D11): a logistic curve in the wider of the F1/F2 bandwidths, times an F0 term from 250 Hz. A token is good when F1 and F2 are both within 10%. AUC (good above poor) 0.78; mean confidence 0.93 against 93.5% good.

| Confidence | Tokens | Good (%) | Mean confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00–0.80 | 67 | 68.7 | 0.59 |
| 0.80–0.90 | 94 | 83.0 | 0.86 |
| 0.90–0.95 | 228 | 93.0 | 0.93 |
| 0.95–1.00 | 709 | 97.5 | 0.97 |

### Resonance score

Per speaker: the score over all their vowels, measured over the nucleus and weighted by formant confidence, against the same score from Hillenbrand's hand-checked formants. Median (10th–90th percentile), and the median / largest difference.

| Group | Speakers | newvoice-core | Hand-checked | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| men | 45 | 0.04 (-0.18–0.29) | 0.00 (-0.22–0.22) | 0.06 / 0.24 |
| women | 48 | 1.03 (0.71–1.27) | 1.01 (0.67–1.29) | 0.07 / 0.32 |

The groups separate almost completely (a woman scores above a man in 99.9% of pairs), as the formula intends: this is the reference data the scale is built on, so it shows the measurement works, not that the score predicts perception. A single token is much noisier (median error 0.14, 90th percentile 0.53), so exercises should score several vowels, as Unit 5's six-vowel criterion does.

### F0

Median F0 over the middle half of the nucleus against Hillenbrand's F0 at the steady-state instant (so some difference is real F0 movement).

| Tracker | Median error (%) | Within 5% (%) | Gross >20% (%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| YIN | 1.3 | 84.7 | 1.70 |
| MPM | 1.3 | 85.3 | 1.61 |
| pYIN | 1.3 | 85.3 | 0.45 |

These vowels exposed a failure the synthetic traps missed: with H4 on F1 (men's /ɝ ʊ ɪ/), YIN and MPM read 3–4× the F0, because the octave guard only looked at the double period. Generalising it to every multiple (decision D11) cut YIN's gross errors here from 4.1% to under 2%. About a third of the remainder are tokens where newvoice-core agrees with Praat and the reference reads differently.

## Known limitations

- **No children or voices above ~300 Hz.** The real vowels are adult men and
  women; formant confidence above 300 Hz follows the synthetic error curve.
- **No real ground truth for running speech.** LibriSpeech is scored against
  Praat; the hand-checked set is isolated h-V-d words.
- **pYIN's voicing** extends into voicing edges and some low-level breath;
  the app's minimum voiced duration and level gate should be kept for
  utterance scores.
- **The telephone channel** costs every tracker about 10% gross errors on
  men's voices; Bluetooth and narrowband capture need a warning (Stage 2).
- **Read American English only.** Both corpora are North American English;
  other languages and dialects need their own norms (Stage 10).
